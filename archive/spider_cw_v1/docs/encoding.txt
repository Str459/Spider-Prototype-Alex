CHROMOSOME ENCODING (part1_ga/gait.py)
======================================

What a chromosome is
--------------------
27 real numbers that decode into a 300-frame gait (300 x 24 joint angles, MATLAB pose order
L1,L2,L3,L4,R4,R3,R2,R1 x coxa yaw / femur pitch / tibia pitch). Every joint follows one sine:

    angle_j(t) = offset_j + amp_j * sin(2*pi*t/300 + leg_phase(leg_j) + joint_phase(j))

  genes 0-11   BASE POSE of the left legs L1..L4 (12 angles). The right legs are the mirror
               image (R_k = L_k with the coxa yaw negated), so these genes give the full 1x24
               base pose; gait.base_pose(chrom) returns it, and amp = 0 decodes to exactly it.
  genes 12-23  swing AMPLITUDE of the same 12 joints, mirrored the same way.
  gene 24  fb  phase lag between neighbouring legs on one side, front to back
               (leg_phase = LR * is_right + fb * position, position L1..L4 = 0..3, R4..R1 = 3..0).
               LR = pi is a constant: the right side is always half a cycle behind (step 4 below).
  gene 25      femur phase relative to its own leg's coxa (shared by all legs).
  gene 26      tibia phase relative to its own leg's coxa (shared by all legs).
Bounds are the joint limits for the pose genes (femur pitch inside (-120, +60) deg, where the
MATLAB kinematics stay defined), 0..0.5 / 0.4 / 0.4 rad for amplitudes, 0..2*pi for phases.
Every gene is a plain real number, so blend / uniform / two-point crossover and Gaussian
mutation all apply unchanged. The 3 phase genes are circular: gait.WRAP marks them so the GA
wraps them modulo 2*pi instead of clipping (0 and 2*pi are the same gait).

Why this design (each step is backed by a measurement)
------------------------------------------------------
1. One sine per joint (instead of 7200 raw angles): a smooth gait that loops seamlessly, and a
   search space small enough to converge in minutes. Kept from the first version.

2. Coupled phases (a metachronal wave) instead of 24 independent phases.
   The first encoding had 72 genes (24 offsets, 24 amplitudes, 24 free phases). The GA stood
   still on every seed. Probes showed why:
     * scaling a hand-built tetrapod's amplitudes by k: score 1.00 (k=0, standing), 0.71 (0.1),
       0.74 (0.25), 1.25 (0.5), 1.81 (1.0). Small strides drag feet before they lift clear, so
       standing is a genuine local optimum (and dragging really is worse than standing).
     * the hand tetrapod's amplitudes with 2000 random sets of 24 phases: median -6.2, none beat
       standing. Walking is a coordination problem; 24 free phases are 24 ways to be uncoordinated.
   Real spiders and insects coordinate legs as a wave: each leg repeats its neighbour's step with
   a fixed lag, and the sides are offset (lr = fb = pi is the alternating tetrapod, smaller fb a
   ripple gait). Encoding the lags directly makes every chromosome a coordinated gait, and it
   still contains the hand tetrapod exactly (fitness.hand_gait: LR=fb=pi, femur=1.5*pi).

3. Bilateral symmetry of the base pose and amplitudes (52 -> 28 genes).
   A stance group of 4 feet only supports the body if all 4 are within the stance tolerance
   (0.05) of the ground at the same time. With 8 independent legs the GA must line up 8 foot
   heights; mirroring halves that and makes a left/right pair level by construction. Bilateral
   symmetry is also what real animals have. Measured (pop 100, 300 generations, final fitness
   without the lift term; "walks" = >= 1.0 per cycle with >= 4 feet down every frame):
       52 genes (wave, no symmetry)     0 / 5 seeds walk  (best 0.60 per cycle)
       28 genes (wave + symmetry)       5 / 8 seeds walk  (1.41 - 3.15 per cycle), 2 stood still
   Not one of 5000 uniformly random 28-gene chromosomes is statically stable, which is why the
   GA's first move is to shrink every amplitude (mean amplitude 0.22 -> 0.05 rad in 40
   generations) and why the "lift" term in the fitness is needed to keep legs stepping.

4. Left and right always alternate (28 -> 27 genes: the lr gene became the constant LR = pi).
   With lr free, all 5 reference seeds converged to lr ~ 0: each left/right pair moved in sync,
   a "rowing" gait that is easy to keep level but is not how a spider walks (very visible when
   played in Webots). Pinning lr = pi, same fitness and settings, seeds 0-4:
       lr free (28 genes)      5 / 5 walk, mean fitness 6.25, all lr ~ 0 (pairs in sync)
       lr = pi (27 genes)      5 / 5 walk, mean fitness 6.88, 4 of 5 fb ~ pi (alternating tetrapod)
   So lr ~ 0 was a search trap (found first, then hard to leave because changing lr alone
   breaks the gait), not what the fitness prefers. Real spiders alternate the two sides.

Final result (27 genes, final fitness, default GAConfig: pop 100, 300 generations;
python -m part1_ga.run_ga --seed S, outputs/part1/seedS):
    seed   progress/cycle   stability   slip    min feet down   min margin   fb
     0         3.61           +0.20     -0.96        4            +0.24      pi    (tetrapod)
     1         2.07           +0.17     -2.26        4            +0.14      0
     2         1.96           +0.15     -2.10        4            +0.11      pi    (tetrapod)
     3         2.40           +0.17     -2.18        4            +0.14      1.4 pi
     4         1.95           +0.18     -2.33        4            +0.16      0.8 pi
All 5 walk with every leg touching down and a real stability margin (0.11-0.24; with lr free
the margins were ~0). Weakness: in seeds 1, 2 and 4 one leg pair stays down all cycle and is
dragged, hence the larger slip.
For comparison the original 72-gene encoding stood still on every seed.

Trade-offs
----------
* Symmetry forbids asymmetric gaits (turning, limping). The goal is straight walking, which a
  symmetric body does with a symmetric posture.
* Fixing LR = pi rules out gaits where a left/right pair moves together (bounding, rowing). That
  is deliberate (step 4): it removes a trap the GA kept falling into, and spiders don't do it.
  fb stays free, so tetrapod (fb = pi), ripple and wave gaits are all still expressible.
* Shared femur/tibia phases mean every leg uses the same stepping pattern, only shifted in
  time. That is what makes it a wave; individual legs still differ in pose and amplitude.
* Going further hurts: sharing the amplitudes too (19 genes: one coxa/femur/tibia amplitude for
  all legs) made all 5 seeds stand still. Any amplitude change then moves every leg at once,
  so no small, stable step towards walking exists.
* One sine per joint cannot draw a flat, straight stance stroke, so some foot slip remains.
