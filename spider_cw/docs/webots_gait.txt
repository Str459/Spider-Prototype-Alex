WEBOTS WALKING GAIT (GA-EVOLVED FOR THE SPIDEROCTOPOD ROBOT)
=============================================================
Files: spider_cw/robot_gait/*.m (MATLAB, GA problem for the robot)
       spider_cw/robot_gait/select_in_webots.py (physics selection)
       controllers/gait_player/gait_player.py (plays + measures a gait)
       worlds/spider_gait.wbt (self-collision ON)
Backup of the previous version: spider_cw/backups/2026-11-15_webots_gait_v1/

1. Robot facts (checked in protos/SpiderOctopod.proto and the world)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
* Forward is +x (legs L1/R1 sit at x = +0.4875). Left legs L1-L4 at +y,
  right legs R1-R4 at -y, hips 0.325 m apart along each side.
* Joints per leg: hip (axis tilted 45 deg, perpendicular to the coxa),
  thigh and knee (about the coxa's x axis). Coxa 0.5 m pointing out and
  45 deg up, thigh 0.6 m, tibia 0.7 m.
* Limits on every leg: hip and thigh +-1.5708 rad, knee [-2, 1] rad.
  Left and right joints are mirrored, so a symmetric gait can only use a
  knee bend of +-1 rad.
* Motors: 2 rad/s and 50 Nm. Mass 43 kg (body 15 kg + 8 x 3.5 kg legs).
* Collision shapes: body box 1.3 x 0.4 x 0.3, coxa cylinder r 0.03,
  thigh box 0.04 x 0.04, tibia cylinder r 0.02. Time step 32 ms.

2. Why the old gait crawled / dragged (measured, not guessed)
-------------------------------------------------------------
The old controller copied the FOOT PATHS of the Part 1 MATLAB gait onto the
robot. Measured in Webots with the same instrumented controller:
    speed 0.11 m/s, planted feet slid 0.65 m per metre walked (3.6 cm per
    step), lowest swing 6.5 cm, body bounce 5.5 mm, tilt up to 2.3 deg.
Causes:
a) Feet on arcs. In the MATLAB model a planted foot is pushed back by
   turning the coxa, i.e. it moves on an arc round the hip. Eight feet on
   eight different arcs cannot all stay still under one rigid body, so
   they have to slide. (The Part 1 fitness already reported ~25-30% slip.)
b) Ripple timing, no overlap. The Part 1 GA chose a front-to-back ripple
   (phaseFB ~ 0.8) with duty exactly 0.5: legs step one after another and
   there is never a moment with both groups down.
c) Start: going from standing straight into the gait dragged all eight
   planted feet to their starting positions at once.

3. What changed
---------------
A GA problem built for THIS robot, using the unchanged Part 1 GA engine
(part1_ga/ga_run.m, tournament selection, two-point crossover, adaptive
mutation, elitism). Part 1 itself is untouched.

Gait structure (robot_gait_decode.m) - the things that make it a walk:
* Two alternating support groups as the starting point:
  A = L1 L3 R2 R4, B = L2 L4 R1 R3. phaseB (delay of B) and wave (front-
  to-back offset) are genes, so the GA could move away from strict
  alternation - it did not (phaseB = 0.497-0.500 in every seed).
* Stance: every planted foot moves in a straight line backwards at the
  same speed -> the body is pushed forwards and no foot needs to slide.
* Swing: the foot lifts with (1 - cos)/2 (zero vertical speed at lift-off
  and touch-down), moves forwards on a cubic curve, and meets the ground
  moving backwards at the stance speed ("ground-speed matching").
* duty > 0.5: both groups are on the ground together for a short time at
  every changeover (the evolved 0.52 gives ~4% of the cycle per change).
* All three joints come from exact inverse kinematics (robot_ik.m) for the
  foot path, so hip, thigh and knee always move together.
* Start-up (robot_gait_startup.m): marching on the spot, then the stride
  grows over three steps. A leg's stride only changes while it is in the
  air, so planted feet never get dragged.

Genes (robot_gait_genes.m), all evolved:
  stride, stepHeight, duty, period, bodyHeight, reach, spread, phaseB,
  wave, xShift, speedMatch.

Fitness (robot_gait_fitness.m), checked on every frame of the cycle:
  goal       forward speed = stride / (duty x period)
  required   foot targets reachable; joints inside limits (0.05 rad margin);
             joint speed <= 1.6 rad/s (80% of motor); static torque <= 42 Nm
             (43 kg shared by the planted feet); neighbouring legs' coxa,
             thigh and tibia >= 4 cm apart (self-collision); >= 4 feet down;
             body centre >= 0.15 m inside the support polygon
  costs      joint acceleration (smoothness); foot ground speed at contact
  standing   stride 0 gives speed 0 PLUS a fixed "not walking" penalty
             (score -5), so standing still can never be selected.

Final selection (select_in_webots.py): every seed's best gait is run in
Webots physics (self-collision on) and scored on MEASURED speed minus
penalties for drift, heading change, tilt, bounce, foot sliding,
self-contacts, low swings and a sunken body. The winner is copied to
outputs/robot_gait/selected/, which the world plays by default.

Tuning done on the way (and why):
* bodyHeight lower bound 0.30 -> 0.42 m: the GA always takes the lowest
  allowed height; 0.42 walked as well as 0.38 in Webots and keeps the
  belly 0.27 m off the floor.
* swingPad gene replaced by speedMatch; sliding measured as the NET
  movement of each planted foot from touch-down to lift-off (summing every
  32 ms increment counted physics jitter as sliding). Tested and rejected:
  half speed and commanding 0.1 s ahead (motor-lag hypothesis) - neither
  reduced sliding.

5. How to run
-------------
Watch it: open worlds/spider_gait.wbt in Webots and press play. The console
prints, every cycle, distance, drift, heading, height, bounce, tilt, the
lowest swing, foot sliding and self-contacts.
Play another candidate: set SpiderOctopod > controllerArgs to "seed2" etc.

Re-evolve and re-select (from spider_cw/robot_gait):
    matlab -batch "test_robot_gait; run_robot_gait_ga(0:4)"
    python select_in_webots.py
