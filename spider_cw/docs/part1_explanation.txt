PART 1 - GENETIC ALGORITHM FOR THE SPIDER GAIT
==============================================

Code is in spider_cw/part1_ga:
  run_ga.m           the GA itself (settings, loop, selection, crossover, mutation) and the plots
  decode_gait.m      turns a chromosome into 300 poses (300 x 24 angles)
  gait_fitness.m     scores a chromosome
  foot_positions.m   forward kinematics (same maths as the lecturer's code, just faster)


1. Chromosome encoding
----------------------
The task asks for a full gait, about 300 poses of 24 angles. Putting all of
those in the chromosome (7200 genes) would be hopeless for a GA, and a random
mix of poses would never look like walking. So the chromosome describes HOW the
legs move, and decode_gait.m builds the 300 poses from it.

The chromosome has 27 real numbers:
  - 6 genes for each pair of legs (front, 2nd, 3rd, back): where the leg points,
    how far it sweeps, femur and tibia angle on the ground, and how much the
    femur and tibia bend when the foot is lifted.
  - phaseLR and phaseFB: the timing between left/right legs and between pairs.
  - duty: how much of the cycle each foot is on the ground.

The left and right legs of a pair share genes (mirrored), so the gait is always
symmetric and we don't need a symmetry term in the fitness.

Each leg does the same step, just delayed by the timing genes. While a foot is
on the ground only the coxa moves. The coxa turns around a vertical axis, so the
foot slides back along the ground but keeps the same height. While the foot is in
the air it comes forward again and the femur/tibia lift it (using sin, so the lift
is 0 when the foot leaves and touches the ground).

We used real numbers rather than binary because the genes are angles, so a small
change in a gene is a small change in the movement, which suits Gaussian mutation.
Every gene has a lower and upper limit (in run_ga.m) to keep the joints in a
sensible range. Genes for the same leg pair are next to each other, so two-point
crossover tends to pass on whole leg pairs.


2. Fitness function
-------------------
There is no physics, so walking is judged from where the feet are.

Reward:
  distance walked per cycle. Feet on the ground are assumed not to slide, so
  when they move back the body moves forward by the same amount.

Penalties (only cost something when a rule is broken):
  - planted feet not at ground height, or any foot under the ground
  - a swinging foot lifted less than 0.15 or more than 0.5
  - unstable frames: fewer than 4 feet down, or the body centre not inside the
    feet. Looking from the body centre, if the planted feet leave a gap of 180
    degrees or more the spider would tip over. We use 150 degrees to leave a margin.
  - neighbouring feet closer than 0.6 (they would hit each other)
  - slip: planted feet moving in different directions (they would have to slide)

At first we also rewarded stability. The GA then found it was easier to stand
still with all feet spread out than to walk, so now distance is the only thing
rewarded and everything else is a rule. The weights are in run_ga.m. The big
ones (unstable 50, below ground 30) are for rules that must never be broken.
The max lift rule was added after the first runs, where the GA kicked the back
legs up almost 2 units high because nothing stopped it.


3. Genetic operators
--------------------
Selection: tournament of 3 (pick 3 at random, the best one is a parent). It only
compares fitness values, so it works with negative fitness, and the tournament
size controls the selection pressure. Roulette wheel is also implemented
(fitness is shifted so all weights are positive).

Crossover: two-point (swap the genes between two random cut points), rate 0.9.
Uniform crossover (each gene swapped with 50% chance) is also implemented.

Mutation: Gaussian. Each gene has a 10% chance of getting a random nudge with a
standard deviation of 10% of that gene's range. Genes are then clipped back into
their limits.

Elitism: the best 2 are copied straight into the next generation, so the best
gait is never lost.


4. Algorithm and parameters
---------------------------
Random population of 60 inside the gene limits -> evaluate -> sort -> keep 2
elites -> fill the rest with selection, crossover and mutation -> repeat.
Stops after 200 generations, or after 40 generations without improvement.
All of these are at the top of run_ga.m. Selection type, crossover type and
the random seed can be passed in, e.g. run_ga('roulette', 'uniform', 2).
One run takes about 15 seconds.


5. Comparing operators
----------------------
Best fitness after a run, 3 seeds each:

  selection    crossover   seed 1   seed 2   seed 3   mean
  tournament   two-point    8.33     9.01     6.07    7.80
  tournament   uniform      5.54     8.93     5.77    6.75
  roulette     two-point    8.09     5.42     4.92    6.14
  roulette     uniform      6.01     5.16     7.17    6.12

Tournament + two-point was best on average, so it is the default. Roulette is
weaker because once most of the population is similar the fitness values are
close together and every chromosome gets nearly the same chance, so there is
little pressure towards the best. Two-point beats uniform here, probably because
it keeps the genes of a leg pair together, while uniform mixes them up.
The results change a lot between seeds, so the GA does get stuck in different
local optima. More seeds or a bigger population would give a fairer comparison.


6. Visualisation (saved in outputs/part1/<settings>/)
------------------------------------------------------
  fitness.png    best and mean fitness per generation. The best line only goes
                 up (elitism). The mean stays below it, which shows the
                 population still has variety.
  feet.png       top: height of every foot over one cycle (on the ground = 0,
                 humps = steps). Bottom: which feet are down in each frame.
  pose.png       frame 1 drawn with the lecturer's plot_spider_pose
  gait.gif       the whole cycle animated with plot_spider_pose
  best_gait.csv  the final gait, 300 x 24 angles in the spec's order
The console also prints progress every 10 generations, and checks
foot_positions.m against the lecturer's forward_leg_kinematics2.m (the
difference is about 1e-15).


7. Limitations
--------------
  - Feet move on arcs around the hip, not straight lines, so there is still
    some slip.
  - Only foot positions are checked for collisions, so leg segments can still
    cross sometimes.
  - Flat ground and straight walking only.
