PART 1 - GA DESIGN AND GENETIC OPERATORS
========================================
Files: part1_ga/ga_run.m (loop), ga_selection.m, ga_crossover.m,
ga_mutation.m, ga_default_config.m (every parameter), run_experiments.m.
The GA is written from scratch; no toolbox is used.

1. The loop (ga_run.m)
----------------------
  initialise  popSize chromosomes, each gene uniform in its bounds
  evaluate    whole population at once (gait_fitness is vectorised)
  repeat:
    sort by fitness, record statistics (for the plots)
    stop if: maxGen reached, OR no improvement for stallGenerations,
             OR best >= targetFitness
    adapt the mutation step sigma
    keep the eliteCount best unchanged                       (elitism)
    pick parents                                  (ga_selection.m)
    recombine pairs of parents                    (ga_crossover.m)
    mutate the children and repair them into bounds (ga_mutation.m)
    evaluate the children; new population = elites + children
The engine knows nothing about spiders: a problem is just bounds, which
genes are circular, and an evaluate function. The same code runs Stage A
(24 genes), Stage B (27 genes) and the sphere test in test_part1.m.
The best chromosome ever seen is stored separately, so it is returned
even when elitism is switched off.

2. Parameters (ga_default_config.m) and why
-------------------------------------------
popSize 100        About 4x the number of genes. Because a whole population
                   is scored in one vectorised call, 250 generations take
                   about 13 s, so there was no need to go smaller.
maxGen 250,        Runs are still creeping upwards at 250 but the gait no
stallGen 80        longer changes visibly (see fitness.png).
eliteCount 2       Guarantees the best never gets lost; small so that two
                   individuals cannot take over the population.
tournament, k=3    See 3.
two-point,         See 4. Chosen from the experiments in section 7.
crossoverRate 0.9
mutationRate 0.15  About 4 of the 27 genes change per child.
sigma0 0.10        Step = 10% of a gene's range at the start.
adaptive sigma     x0.98 after every improving generation, x1.3 after
in [0.01, 0.15]    every 15 stalled generations.
seed               Every run is repeatable.
Every parameter can be overridden by name: run_gait_ga('popSize', 200).

3. Selection (ga_selection.m)
-----------------------------
Tournament (default): draw k = 3 chromosomes at random, keep the best.
* Uses only the ORDER of scores. Our fitness is often negative (early
  chromosomes are mostly penalties) and its scale changes during a run;
  tournament does not care.
* Pressure is set with one number: k = 2 is gentle, k = 5 greedy.
* Cheap and fully vectorised.
Roulette wheel (option): probability proportional to fitness. Needs the
scores shifted to be positive ("windowing": subtract the worst). Weak
point: when everyone has a similar score, everyone gets a similar slice,
so pressure fades exactly when fine-tuning matters. On the sphere test
(test_part1.m) it finished at 0.14 against tournament's 0.019.
Rank (option): probability proportional to rank. Keeps roulette's idea
without its scale problem (sphere test: 0.025).

4. Crossover (ga_crossover.m)
-----------------------------
Two-point crossover (default): pick two cut points and swap the block of
genes between them. Our genes are grouped per leg pair (6 genes each, then
the 3 timing genes), so a block usually carries one or more complete leg
settings from one parent to the child. A leg that already works is passed
on intact instead of being averaged with a different one. It won the
experiments in section 7 (11.61 vs 11.12 for blend).
Blend crossover BLX-alpha (option, the original default): each child gene
is uniform in the parents' interval widened by alpha = 0.3 on both sides.
* Genes are real numbers. Children can land BETWEEN their parents (a stride
  half-way between two good strides), which copying operators cannot do.
* alpha > 0 lets children land a little outside the parents too, so the
  population does not shrink just from recombining.
* Phase genes are circular: the second parent's phase is first moved by a
  whole cycle if that is closer, so 0.95 and 0.05 blend to around 0.0, not
  0.5.
Uniform (option): each gene from either parent, 50/50.
One-point (option): one cut, swap everything after it.
crossoverRate 0.9: one pair in ten is copied unchanged, giving good parents
a chance to survive into the next generation besides the elites.

5. Mutation (ga_mutation.m)
---------------------------
Each gene mutates with probability 0.15 by adding Gaussian noise with
standard deviation sigma x (gene range). Scaling by the range makes one
sigma mean the same for a phase gene (range 1) and a tibia gene (range 2).
Afterwards genes are repaired: phases wrap round, others are clipped.

Adaptive sigma: big steps explore early, small steps fine-tune later.
* Shrink x0.98 after an improvement.
* Grow x1.3 after 15 generations without one, to shake the population out
  of a local optimum.
* Tuning evidence: the first version allowed sigma up to 0.30 and grew it
  x1.5 every 10 stalled generations. After a stall it pushed sigma to the
  cap and the mean fitness fell from about 10 to 0 while the best stayed
  flat: the population was being scattered at random rather than explored
  near the best. Capping sigma at 0.15 and growing more gently fixed this
  (compare outputs/part1/seed*/fitness.png).

6. Results with the default settings (seeds 0-4)
------------------------------------------------
Tournament selection (k=3), two-point crossover, adaptive mutation:
seed  fitness  distance/cycle  min margin  fewest feet down  phaseLR  phaseFB
  0    11.60       4.27           0.43            4            0.50     0.80
  1    11.20       4.11           0.40            4            0.50     0.77
  2    11.57       4.28           0.28            4            0.50     0.36
  3    12.07       4.39           0.31            4            0.59     0.75
  4    11.62       4.24           0.46            4            0.54     0.75
All five seeds walk (target was >= 1.0 per cycle with >= 4 feet down in
every frame). Each run takes about 13 s of GA time on one core.
(With the original blend crossover the same seeds scored 10.84-11.50 and
walked 3.92-4.21 per cycle.)

What the GA discovered by itself:
* phaseLR settles at or near 0.5: the left and right legs of a pair step
  in antiphase, as in real spiders.
* phaseFB settles near 0.75-0.80 in four seeds and 0.36 in one: a wave of
  steps running along each side, with the two sides alternating.
* duty sits at its lower bound 0.5: exactly 4 feet down at any moment,
  which is the minimum allowed, leaving the longest time to swing.
* coxaSwing sits at its upper bound 0.5 for every pair in every seed:
  bigger strides always pay while collisions are avoided. The bound, not
  the fitness, limits stride length. Widening it is a possible extension.
* coxaOffset is -0.45 to -0.5 (front pair) and +0.5 (rear pair) in every
  seed: the front legs are swung back and the rear legs forward, i.e. both
  turned towards the sides, which reduces slip (see part1_fitness.txt). The
  middle pairs mostly follow the same pattern less strongly (about -0.24
  and +0.21); seed 1 keeps them straight out (0.00 and +0.02).
Different seeds find different phaseFB values with similar fitness, i.e.
the landscape has several good optima, not one.

7. Experiments: one component changed at a time (run_experiments.m)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Each variant changes ONE setting from the baseline; 5 seeds each, 250
generations. Plots: outputs/part1/experiments/convergence.png and
final_scores.png. Raw numbers: summary.csv, runs.csv.

The baseline here is the ORIGINAL default (blend crossover); the default
was switched to two-point because of this table.

variant          fitness (mean +- std)   distance/cycle   time/run
baseline (blend)   11.12 +- 0.33           4.03            14 s
roulette            7.64 +- 1.03           3.01            13 s
rank                8.83 +- 1.25           3.43            11 s
tournament k=5     11.20 +- 0.25           4.03            12 s
uniform            11.35 +- 0.31           4.10            12 s
two-point          11.61 +- 0.31           4.26            13 s
no crossover        9.34 +- 0.70           3.53            14 s
fixed sigma        10.55 +- 0.21           3.90            13 s
no elitism         10.16 +- 0.57           3.83            14 s
pop 50             10.00 +- 0.69           3.78             5 s
pop 200            10.12 +- 1.13           3.81            26 s
All 55 runs produced a walking gait (>= 1.0 per cycle, >= 4 feet down in
every frame, never unstable), so the encoding + fitness are robust; the
components decide HOW GOOD the walk gets.

What this shows
* Selection matters most. Tournament beat roulette by 3.5 fitness points
  (about 1 unit of distance per cycle). Roulette loses pressure once the
  population's scores are close, exactly as on the sphere test. Rank sits
  in between. k = 5 gives the same mean as k = 3 with less spread.
* Crossover clearly helps: switching it off costs 1.8 points. Two-point
  crossover was the best variant overall (11.61), ahead of uniform (11.35)
  and blend (11.12). Our genes are grouped per leg pair, so swapping
  contiguous blocks passes a whole working leg setting to the child, which
  helps more here than blending individual values. Two-point is now the
  default, and the seed results in section 6 use it.
* Adaptive mutation beats a fixed sigma (11.12 vs 10.55). Fixed sigma has
  the smallest spread: it is consistent but cannot fine-tune.
* Elitism matters: without it the best gait is regularly lost and
  re-found, ending 1 point lower.
* Population size: 100 was best for a fixed 250 generations. 50 has too
  little variety; 200 converges more slowly (with tournament size 3 the
  pressure is weaker in a bigger population) and costs twice the time, so
  it needs more generations to pay off.
