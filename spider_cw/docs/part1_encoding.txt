PART 1 - CHROMOSOME ENCODING
============================
Files: part1_ga/gait_genes.m, part1_ga/gait_decode.m (Stage B, the gait)
       part1_ga/run_pose_ga.m (Stage A, a single pose)

1. Two stages, one GA engine
----------------------------
Stage A (run_pose_ga.m): the chromosome is exactly the 1x24 vector the
provided plot_spider_pose.m accepts:
    [L1a L1b L1c L2a ... R1a R1b R1c]   (radians, a = coxa, b = femur, c = tibia)
The GA evolves one stable standing pose. This is the most literal reading of
"find a valid chromosome of 24 angles" and shows the operators working on a
problem whose answer you can judge by eye.

Stage B (run_gait_ga.m): the spec asks for a full gait of about 300 such
vectors. Here the chromosome is 27 real numbers that DESCRIBE a gait;
gait_decode.m turns them into the 300 x 24 matrix, one spec-format pose per
row (best_gait.csv). Both stages use the same ga_run.m.

2. Why not put all 300 x 24 = 7200 angles in the chromosome?
------------------------------------------------------------
* The search space would be enormous and almost every random point is a
  spider twitching, not walking. Crossover between two such chromosomes
  mixes frames from unrelated motions.
* Nothing would force frame 300 to join back up with frame 1, or
  neighbouring frames to be close to each other.
A gait is periodic and smooth, so we encode the parameters of a periodic,
smooth motion instead. The decoder guarantees continuity and looping; the GA
only has to discover the shape and timing.

3. Why not one sine wave per joint (offset, amplitude, phase x 24 = 72 genes)?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
An earlier prototype in this project did exactly that. Every seed converged
to standing still. The reasons were measured:
* Standing is a strong local optimum. Scaling a working hand-made stride from
  0 to full size, the score first DROPS (1.00 -> 0.71 -> 0.74) before rising
  (-> 1.25 -> 1.81): small strides drag the feet along the ground before the
  lift is big enough to clear it.
* Timing is the needle in the haystack. With good amplitudes but random
  phases, the median score was -6.2 and none of 2000 samples beat standing.
  24 independent phases almost never line up into a usable stepping pattern.

4. The encoding we use (27 genes)
---------------------------------
Genes 1-24: six per front-to-back pair of legs (front, midfront, midrear,
rear). The left and right leg of a pair share them, mirrored:
    coxaOffset   [-0.5, 0.5]  mean coxa angle, + = foot further forward
    coxaSwing    [ 0.0, 0.5]  coxa amplitude (half the stride)
    femurStance  [-1.2, 0.6]  femur angle while the foot is planted
    femurLift    [ 0.0, 0.8]  extra femur angle at the top of a swing
    tibiaStance  [-2.0, 0.0]  tibia angle while the foot is planted
    tibiaLift    [-0.8, 0.8]  extra tibia angle at the top of a swing
Genes 25-27: timing shared by every leg:
    phaseLR      [0, 1)       delay of a right leg behind its left partner
    phaseFB      [0, 1)       delay of each pair behind the pair in front
    duty         [0.5, 0.9]   fraction of the cycle a foot is planted

How the decoder builds a step (gait_decode.m)
* Leg delay = (pair - 1) * phaseFB (+ phaseLR on the right). This is the
  "metachronal wave" seen in real spiders and insects: legs step in a wave
  running along the body, and the two sides alternate. Two numbers describe
  the whole pattern, instead of 24 independent phases. phaseLR = 0.5 gives
  the alternating tetrapod gait (L1 R2 L3 R4 / R1 L2 R3 L4).
* STANCE (foot planted, fraction "duty" of the cycle): only the coxa moves,
  sweeping the foot backwards at constant speed. A coxa turn is a rotation
  about the vertical axis, so it cannot change the foot's height. If the
  femur/tibia stance angles put the foot on the ground, it STAYS on the
  ground for the whole stance. This removes the "small strides drag the
  feet" valley that trapped the sine-wave prototype.
* SWING (foot in the air): the coxa brings the foot forward along a smooth
  cosine; femur and tibia add a half-sine lift that is zero at lift-off and
  touch-down, so there is no jump in any joint angle.

Evidence it works (test_part1.m, check [4]): a hand-made walk in this
encoding scores 3.08, marching on the spot 0.28, so stride is rewarded
straight away. The hand-made walk is only used in that test; it is never put
into the GA population (that would make the GA pointless).

5. Design choices and trade-offs
--------------------------------
* Real-valued genes, not binary. Joint angles are continuous. Real genes
  avoid "Hamming cliffs" (in binary, 0111 -> 1000 is a tiny change in value
  but needs every bit to flip) and allow blend crossover to create children
  BETWEEN their parents.
* Mirrored pairs halve the genes (24 instead of 48 per-leg values) and make
  the gait symmetric by construction, so the fitness needs no symmetry term.
  Cost: this encoding cannot learn to turn. Dropping the mirroring would
  allow turning at the price of a harder search.
* Circular phase genes. Phase 0.95 and 0.05 are 0.1 apart, not 0.9. Mutation
  wraps them round (ga_mutation.m) and blend crossover moves the second
  parent by a whole cycle when that brings it closer (ga_crossover.m).
* Bounds come from the geometry: a coxa swing of +-0.5 rad (+-29 deg) roughly
  matches the 30 deg spacing between neighbouring legs, and the femur/tibia
  ranges cover every way of putting a foot on the ground at z = -0.4.
* The genes are grouped per leg pair, so block (one/two-point) crossover
  tends to keep one leg's settings together.
* The encoding cannot express everything: stance-phase femur/tibia motion is
  fixed, so feet move in arcs rather than straight lines (see the slip
  discussion in part1_fitness.txt).
